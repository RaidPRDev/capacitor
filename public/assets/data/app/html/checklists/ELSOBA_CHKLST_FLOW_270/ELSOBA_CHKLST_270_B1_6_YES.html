• Pass a new sterile sash into the field: sterile tubing primed with a syringe on the field, with the clamped ends passed out to connect to the recirculated circuit OR pass one end out and prime it from the circuit while the surgeon holds the open end up, clamps above the fluid meniscus and passes the clamped end out to connect.
  • Recirculate through the new sash, de-air and check a circuit gas.
  • Manage sweep during priming per institutional protocol (sweep on with CO2 bleed-in, or sweep off until flow is started).
  • Gradually start ECMO flow and sweep gas flow.
  • Gradually wean from CPB, keeping the expected oxygen delivery consistent throughout.
  • Verify that the target extracorporeal blood flow is achieved.
  • Adjust ECMO gas blender settings as necessary.
  • Adjust heater unit / heater-cooler unit water temperature as necessary.
Critical: Once full ECMO flow is achieved, clamp the CPB lines and stop the pump.
  • Carefully remove the CPB cannulas and repair the insertion sites as needed.
Critical: Do not fully reverse anticoagulation. Partial heparin reversal may be used; see below.
Partial reversal of heparin (ELSO Blue Book)
  • Protamine ⅓ dose (1 mg/kg) can be given.
  • Wait 5 minutes and draw an ACT.
  • If the ACT exceeds 250 seconds, give another 1 mg/kg and repeat the ACT.
  • Continue until the ACT is under 250 seconds.
  • Ensure a backup circuit is instantly available.
  • Anesthesiology can now start platelets, FFP, cryoprecipitate and prothrombin complex concentrates.
  • RBCs can be given into the circuit if rapid administration is needed; give clotting factors and platelets post-membrane lung.
  • Salvage the blood from the CPB circuit: the perfusionist flushes it into a bowl in the surgical field, where it is sucked into the cell saver and processed.
  • Keep the CPB circuit clear-primed as a backup (per institutional protocol).
Critical: Do not restart anticoagulation for at least 6 hours or until bleeding is controlled.
  • Dress the insertion sites and adjust the tubing to the desired configuration.
This concludes the Central VA ECMO to Central CPB or Peripheral VA ECMO to Peripheral CPB algorithm.
